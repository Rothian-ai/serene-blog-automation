Off-plan service charges in Dubai are owed from the point a building is complete and the unit is yours to take, not from the day you choose to collect the keys. While the tower is under construction there is nothing to maintain and no approved budget, so the developer carries the running costs, and it keeps paying for every unit it has not sold. Once the project is complete, the [Dubai Rental Disputes Center has held](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-rental-disputes-center-clarifies-who-pays-property-service-charges-before-handover-1.500291650) that a buyer on the preliminary register owes service charges from the earlier of two dates: the project's completion date, or the date the buyer defaulted on payments. A buyer who delays handover by missing the final instalment does not delay the bill.

The amount is harder. No law requires a developer to give a buyer a service charge estimate at booking, and any figure in a brochure is not a budget. The real number is set later, by a budget that the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) must approve. Until then, the best estimate comes from comparable completed buildings in the Dubai Land Department's own index, used with care.

## What the law says about off-plan service charges

The framework is [Law No. 6 of 2019 on jointly owned property](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2019/Law%20No.%20%286%29%20of%202019%20Concerning%20Ownership%20of%20Jointly%20Owned%20Real%20Property%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.html). Four of its provisions decide most of what an off-plan buyer will meet.

| Provision | What it says | What it means for a buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Article 25(a) | Each owner pays a share of the annual service charges based on the unit's area; the developer pays for unsold units, and for sold units where the sale contract says it will | The share follows floor area. A payment plan that "includes" service charges for a period is a contractual promise by the developer, and should be written into the SPA |
| Article 27 | No amount may be collected without RERA's prior approval; the budget must be audited; RERA may approve a temporary budget meanwhile | A charge with no RERA approval behind it can be questioned. The first budget may be a temporary one |
| Article 30(d) | Until RERA appoints a management company, the developer manages the property and keeps owners' charges in the designated account | In the early years the developer, or its own management company, usually sets and spends the budget |
| Article 32 | The management entity has a lien on every unit for unpaid charges; the unit cannot be sold until they are paid, and an execution judge may order a sale by auction | Arrears follow the unit. A resale or a mortgage at handover will expose them |

Article 28 closes the obvious loophole: an owner may not refuse to pay charges that RERA has approved, whatever the dispute with the developer about snagging or delay. Those disputes have their own routes. The service charge account is kept separate.

## When the bill starts, stage by stage

The useful way to read the law is as a sequence. The buyer's exposure grows as the building moves from drawings to an occupied tower.

| Stage | Who pays the running costs | What figure exists |
|---|---|---|
| Booking and construction | The developer, as nothing is yet in use | At most a developer's indication. No budget |
| Completion | The buyer, from the completion date (or from an earlier payment default) | Often a temporary budget approved by RERA |
| Handover | The buyer, usually with charges collected in advance and a reserve fund contribution | The temporary or first audited budget |
| Owners' association formed | Owners, through an approved budget and audited accounts | Audited budgets, published in the index |

At handover, [buyers are commonly asked for service charges in advance](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/03/27/uae-property-what-all-are-buyers-expected-to-pay-at-the-time-of-property-handover), along with an initial reserve fund contribution, utility deposits and, where the building has one, chiller registration. The National's guide, written by a broker, gives no fixed amounts because they vary by building; it notes that completion costs on a unit of around AED 1.5 million can run into tens of thousands of dirhams. Budget for the advance as part of the final payment, not after it.

## How to estimate the charge before a budget exists

Every RERA-approved budget is published in the [Dubai Land Department's Service Charge Index](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/service-charge-index/), which can be searched by title deed, by project or by map. For a building that is not yet complete, the index has nothing to show. The method is to find completed buildings that resemble the one you are buying and read their approved rates.

The index states each building's approved budget as AED per square foot of chargeable area per year. [Continental Club's compilation of the index](https://continentalclub.ae/dubai-service-charge-index/), drawn from RERA-approved budgets in Mollak and updated on 10 November 2026, gives a median of AED 15.54 per square foot a year for residential buildings across 1,614 budgets in 2026. It is a third-party summary of official data, refreshed weekly, so the median moves slightly from one week to the next. Use the official index for the building itself.

## Why a community median is not your building's number

Look at the lowest and highest columns before the median. In Jumeirah Village Circle, approved rates run from AED 1.28 to AED 42.66 per square foot. In Business Bay, from AED 4.94 to AED 53.95. A median describes the middle of a community. It says almost nothing about one tower within it.

The spread has ordinary causes. A building with several pools, landscaped podiums and a concierge costs more to run than one with a gym and a lobby, a point the [same broker makes in a separate column](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/09/26/uae-property-are-you-paying-too-much-in-service-charges-for-your-apartment/) on whether owners are overpaying. Some buildings add district cooling or chiller charges that sit outside the headline rate. And the rate is the approved budget divided across the building's chargeable area. It is not the invoice for any particular unit.

So the comparison that works is narrow: completed buildings of a similar age, by the same developer where possible, with a similar list of amenities, in the same part of the community. Three or four of those, read from the index, give a sounder range than any citywide or community figure.

## The first budget is rarely the settled one

A new building starts with no operating history. Under Article 27, RERA may approve a temporary budget until an audited one is ready, and the developer manages the property until a management company is appointed. The first figures are therefore estimates made by the party that also sold the units.

That is not a sign of bad faith. It is a reason to treat the first year's charge as provisional and to plan for it to change once real running costs are known, in either direction. Owners who think a charge has been set without approval have a sequence open to them, set out in The National's column: ask the developer whether a temporary budget was approved and for its Mollak reference, then, if the answer does not settle it, raise the matter with the Dubai Land Department or RERA.

## Questions to settle before you sign

The SPA and the developer's documents can answer more than most buyers ask of them.

- **Does the SPA say who pays service charges, and from when?** If a payment plan promises a period free of charges, Article 25(a) recognises that only where the contract states it.
- **Which comparable completed buildings does the developer manage, and what are their approved rates in the index?** A developer's existing towers are the closest evidence of how it budgets.
- **Is there district cooling, and is it billed separately?** It can be a meaningful cost outside the service charge.
- **What will be collected at handover?** Ask how many months of charges in advance, and the size of the reserve fund contribution.
- **What is the chargeable area of the unit?** The charge follows area, so the figure used for the bill matters as much as the rate.

## What this changes in the decision

Service charges are a running cost that lasts as long as ownership does, and on an off-plan purchase they arrive at the point when the buyer's cash is most stretched. Two units at a similar price in the same community can carry approved rates many times apart, as the range in Jumeirah Village Circle shows. That difference belongs in the comparison between projects, alongside the payment plan and the handover date, and it can only be judged from completed buildings, approved budgets and the contract, read before the booking rather than after the keys.
