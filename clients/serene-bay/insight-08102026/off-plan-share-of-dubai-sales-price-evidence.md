In the second quarter of 2026, off-plan homes made up 76 per cent of Dubai's residential sales, up from 68 per cent in the first quarter, according to [betterhomes' Q2 2026 market report](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/q2-2026-sales-cooled-to-aed849bn-but-prices-and-rents-kept-climbing). The off-plan share of Dubai sales rose for an unflattering reason: resale activity fell far harder than new-launch activity, so the mix tilted. Off-plan did not boom.

The practical consequence for a buyer is about evidence, not enthusiasm. When three in four sales are of homes that do not yet exist, the resale market, which is the usual check on whether a launch price is fair, becomes thin. A thin comparison set is easier to misread, and easier to be misled by. A buyer should widen what they compare against rather than lean on the headline average.

## The off-plan share of Dubai sales in Q2 2026

The figures below are from betterhomes' report, which draws on Dubai Land Department (DLD) registrations. Total residential value was AED 84.9 billion, [down 45 per cent year on year](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/q2-2026-sales-cooled-to-aed849bn-but-prices-and-rents-kept-climbing).

| Q2 2026 | Transactions | Share of sales | Change year on year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off-plan | 26,338 | 76% | down 12% |
| Secondary (resale) | 8,512 | 24% | down 59% |
| Total residential | 34,850 | 100% | down 31% |

Source: [betterhomes, Q2 2026 Dubai residential market report](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/q2-2026-sales-cooled-to-aed849bn-but-prices-and-rents-kept-climbing).

Totals differ by publisher because scope and method differ. [Khaleej Times reported Dh87.9 billion](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-abu-dhabi-property-markets-resilient-regional-conflict) for the same quarter and a smaller fall in resale volumes than betterhomes did. The direction agrees across sources even where the decimals do not. Quote a share, not a false precision.

## Why the share rose

A fall in resale is not the same as a rise in off-plan. Off-plan transactions in fact fell by 12 per cent. The share moved because the denominator shrank.

[betterhomes' own explanation](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/betterinformed/off-plan-hits-76-of-dubais-market-heres-whats-behind-it) is structural. A developer needs to keep selling to keep a project funded and on schedule, which sets a floor under off-plan activity. A resale seller can wait, hold the price or withdraw the listing. Off-plan has no such option. Note the source: betterhomes sells off-plan property, so treat its reading as one informed view and not a neutral audit.

## How a thin resale market weakens price evidence

Off-plan prices are asking prices set by the seller of a product that has no resale history. The best independent check is what comparable completed homes in the same area actually change hands for. That check works when the resale market is active. With 8,512 resale deals across the whole emirate in a quarter, many communities will have a handful of comparables, or none from the past three months.

Three things follow.

- **Averages hide the mix.** A citywide price per square foot moves with which communities and unit types sold, not only with what prices did. betterhomes noted that [price per square foot rose across most tracked communities](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/q2-2026-sales-cooled-to-aed849bn-but-prices-and-rents-kept-climbing), led by Palm Jumeirah Garden Homes, while off-plan sales above AED 15 million rose 27 per cent year on year even as total prime deals fell 59 per cent. Those are different markets inside one number.
- **Registration lags agreement.** DLD records a sale when it is registered, so a quarter's figures partly reflect agreements made earlier.
- **Payment plans are part of the price.** A lower upfront share is a financing term. It does not make the unit cheaper, and it can make two launches look closer in price than they are.

## What a buyer can use instead

When resale comparables are sparse, the evidence has to come from several places at once.

| Question | Where the answer sits |
|---|---|
| What do completed homes nearby sell for? | DLD transaction records for the building or community, not a citywide average |
| What does the developer's earlier phase now resell for? | Resale of the developer's own handed-over projects |
| What is the plan actually worth? | The payment schedule converted into a single total cost, including fees |
| Is the developer delivering? | Its record against its own milestone dates, covered in our look at [how late off-plan projects run](/insights/how-late-dubai-off-plan-projects-run) |

The second row matters most in practice. A developer that has already handed over a neighbouring phase has produced the one thing a launch cannot: a resale price for its own finished product. If those homes resell below what the earlier buyers paid, or do not resell at all, that is more informative than any average. If they resell above, the premium is worth understanding before paying for the next phase.

None of this needs a forecast. It needs the buyer to ask what the number was measured against.

## Reading the share arithmetically

A share is a ratio, so it can rise while its numerator falls. Working backwards from the table gives a useful check. If off-plan sales fell 12 per cent to 26,338, the same quarter a year earlier held roughly 29,900 off-plan sales. If resale fell 59 per cent to 8,512, it held roughly 20,800. On those numbers off-plan was about 59 per cent of sales a year earlier (our arithmetic from betterhomes' published changes, not a figure the report states).

So the move from roughly 59 to 76 per cent is real, but it is mostly a story about resale leaving the market. Anyone quoting "off-plan now dominates" as a sign of rising demand for new launches has read the ratio the wrong way round. Anyone quoting it as a sign of distress has made the opposite error: off-plan volumes fell by about one-eighth, which is a softer quarter, not a collapse.

## What the shift does not change

A high off-plan share is not a warning on its own, and it is not an endorsement. Launches still differ in location, developer record, handover timeline and payment terms, as betterhomes itself acknowledges. Funds still sit in a regulated escrow account and are released against construction progress, a mechanism we set out in [our explainer on escrow](/insights/escrow-explained-what-rera-holds).

What changes is the weight any single data point can bear. A quarter in which the resale market contracts by 59 per cent says something about resale sentiment. It says much less about whether a particular tower is priced fairly. That question is answered unit by unit, against several comparison sets, by someone with no reason to prefer one launch over another.

## Questions to put to any comparison

Whoever presents a price comparison, whether a developer, a portal or an advisor, can be asked the same four things. What period does it cover? How many completed sales sit behind it? Is it a citywide figure or the specific community? Does the price include the cost of the payment plan? A comparison that cannot answer all four is an opinion with a chart attached.
