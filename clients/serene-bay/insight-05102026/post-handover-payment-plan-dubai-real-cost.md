A post-handover payment plan is not free credit. It is a loan from the developer, and the interest is folded into the price of the unit. [Property trackers define the structure](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/post-handover-payment-plan-what-it-means) as deferring 20 to 50 per cent of the purchase price into the one to five years after completion at no stated interest. The absence of a stated rate is the point to examine. Developers who offer a tail usually price the unit above the cash or construction-linked price, and the gap is the rate.

So the useful question is not whether a plan is attractive, but what it costs per year. Take the premium over the cash price of the same unit, and divide it across the average time the money is deferred. A 10 per cent premium on money held back for three years is an implied rate of about 3.2 per cent a year. The same premium on money held for 18 months is about 6.6 per cent. Both can be reasonable. Neither is zero.

This matters because off-plan is the main way Dubai buys. In June 2026 it accounted for [76 per cent of residential activity, 9,442 transactions worth Dh17.6 billion](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-homes-record-dh2213b-sales-in-first-half-of-2026-1.500598014), according to Gulf News reporting of Dubai Land Department data. Most buyers will be offered a schedule. Few are shown what it costs.

## What a post-handover payment plan actually is

During construction the buyer pays against milestones, commonly in a 60/40 or 70/30 pattern, with the balance due at handover. A post-handover plan moves part of that balance past the handover date and into instalments, monthly or quarterly, over a fixed term. The buyer receives the keys, and the Oqood registration converts to a title deed, while a portion of the price is still owed.

Plans are a minority product. The [VILA Properties project register](https://vila.ae/dubai-off-plan-payment-plans-2026/) showed 1,093 live off-plan projects on 5 October 2026, with a median entry price of AED 1.79 million and a median down payment of 15 per cent. Of those projects, 17 per cent offered a post-handover plan.

## Where the premium sits

A developer cannot lend for free, so the cost is recovered somewhere. It appears in one of three places: a higher headline price, a smaller discount for paying early, or a lower specification. The first two are visible if you ask for the right quotes. The same VILA guide reports that [20/80 plans are typically priced 8 to 12 per cent above cash terms, and that 90/10 or cash terms typically carry a discount of 5 to 10 per cent](https://vila.ae/dubai-off-plan-payment-plans-2026/).

Read those two ranges together. The distance between the cheapest cash price and the dearest extended plan on a comparable unit can approach 20 per cent. That is the real spread a buyer is choosing within.

## Turning the premium into an annual rate

The arithmetic is simple compounding: the implied annual rate equals one plus the premium, raised to the power of one over the years deferred, minus one. The table applies it to the premium range above. It is our own calculation, not a published statistic, and it assumes the whole premium is paid for the whole deferral period.

| Premium over cash price | Deferred 1.5 years | Deferred 3 years | Deferred 4 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 per cent | 3.3 per cent a year | 1.6 per cent a year | 1.2 per cent a year |
| 8 per cent | 5.3 per cent a year | 2.6 per cent a year | 1.9 per cent a year |
| 10 per cent | 6.6 per cent a year | 3.2 per cent a year | 2.4 per cent a year |
| 12 per cent | 7.8 per cent a year | 3.8 per cent a year | 2.9 per cent a year |

Two things follow. A longer tail dilutes a given premium, so a four-year plan at 10 per cent is cheaper per year than an 18-month plan at 5 per cent. And a premium that looks small on the brochure can be dear if the deferral it buys is short. The right comparison is always the rate, set against what your own money would earn or cost elsewhere.

## A worked example

Take a unit with a cash price of AED 1,500,000, and a plan priced 10 per cent higher at AED 1,650,000. The extra AED 150,000 is the cost of borrowing. If 50 per cent of the plan price is deferred over three years after handover, the buyer is financing AED 825,000 at an effective cost that is the one in the table above, roughly 3 per cent a year on a straight reading. If instead the same premium buys only 18 months of deferral, the rate is double.

The example uses round numbers on purpose. The method works with any figures, and an advisor can run it on the actual price list of each project in a few minutes.

## Costs that sit outside the price

The premium is not the only cost of the tail. Service charges begin once the building is occupied or handed over, and [trackers put them at AED 10 to AED 25 per square foot a year](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/post-handover-payment-plan-what-it-means) during the post-handover period. A utility deposit and fit-out follow. If the unit is let, rent has to cover the instalment as well as the charges, and a plan that looks affordable on paper can turn negative on a one-bedroom. The same source records a median new-lease rent of AED 72,000 for a Dubai one-bedroom on 2026 Ejari contracts, against AED 75,000 on 2025 contracts, so rent is not guaranteed to rise to meet the instalment.

## Risks the buyer keeps

Three risks stay with the buyer through the tail, and a price premium does not remove any of them.

- **Counterparty risk in reverse.** During construction the buyer's money sits in escrow. After handover, the buyer owes the developer, and the contract sets out what happens on a missed instalment. Read the default clause before the price.
- **Resale friction.** Contracts commonly make transfer of a unit with an outstanding balance subject to the developer, and a buyer for the unit takes on the schedule or settles it. That narrows the pool of buyers.
- **Valuation gap.** A valuer prices the unit, not the plan. A premium paid for deferral may not be reflected in a valuation, so the price paid can sit above where the market sees the unit.

## Questions to put to every plan

Ask for the cash price of the same unit, in writing, on the same date as the plan price. Ask what share of the premium falls on each instalment. Ask for the default and early-settlement terms. Ask whether the developer will accept early repayment at par, because a plan that can be cleared early at no penalty has a rate you can end. Then compare the implied rate with what the capital would do if it were not tied up.

A cross-developer comparison makes this easier, because the same buyer can set an 18-month plan from one developer against a four-year plan from another and see the rate each implies. That comparison is the substance of salaried advice: the figures are the same whoever presents them, and the work is in asking for them.
