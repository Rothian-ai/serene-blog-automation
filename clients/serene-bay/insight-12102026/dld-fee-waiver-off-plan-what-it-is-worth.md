A DLD fee waiver is worth exactly 4 per cent of the unit price, and no more. On a median off-plan apartment that is roughly AED 77,645, and on a median villa roughly AED 271,655, according to [Betterhomes' analysis of 2026 median off-plan prices](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-news/dubai-developers-bring-back-4-dld-waivers-and-buyer-incentives). It is a real saving, paid once, at registration. It is also a developer promotion rather than a government scheme, and it leaves the sale price untouched. That second point is the one buyers tend to miss. A waiver and a price reduction of the same size are not the same offer, because everything that is later measured against the price (a mortgage valuation, a resale ask, a comparison with the next tower along) still starts from the higher number.

This piece sets out what the fee is, why developers reach for the waiver when volumes soften, how to put a number on it, and what to check before treating it as the deciding factor.

## What the 4 per cent actually is

The Dubai Land Department charges a registration fee on a sale. For an initial (off-plan) sale, [DLD's own service page](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-to-register-the-initial-sale/) describes the fee as split evenly: 2 per cent from the seller and 2 per cent from the purchaser, plus small fixed knowledge and innovation fees of AED 10 each. In practice the market treats it as a 4 per cent cost sitting on the buyer. A [DLD announcement on developer remittance](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/dld-calls-on-developers-to-pay-property-registration-fees-within-60-days/) confirms the mechanics: the buyer settles the registration fee with the developer, and the developer passes it to DLD within 60 days.

That routing is why a waiver is possible at all. The developer collects the fee from you and remits it, so the developer can choose to absorb it. The buyer pays less cash at the point of purchase. DLD still receives its revenue.

## Why waivers return when volumes fall

Off-plan remains the largest part of Dubai's residential market, but the market around it has cooled. [Cavendish Maxwell data reported by The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/10/07/dubai-home-sale-transactions-fall-in-third-quarter-due-to-regional-uncertainty/) puts off-plan at 65 per cent of residential sales value and 72 per cent of purchases in the third quarter of 2026, while total sales value fell 47 per cent year on year to AED 72.6 billion and transactions fell 38 per cent to 34,000.

A broker release carried by [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-studies/dubai-real-estate-transactions-reach-aed-90.62bln-in-q3-as-off-plan-remains-the-largest-residential-segment-1510107) gives the same quarter in counts: 23,457 off-plan residential transactions against 10,442 in the secondary market. Treat that release as a broker's tally rather than a DLD publication, since the sources differ slightly on totals.

The pattern is a familiar one. When fewer buyers are transacting, a developer that does not want to cut headline prices (which would affect every unit already sold and every comparable it is trying to hold) reaches for an incentive that leaves the price list intact. A fee waiver does exactly that. So does a softer payment plan. [Betterhomes lists](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-news/dubai-developers-bring-back-4-dld-waivers-and-buyer-incentives) waiver offers across projects from Binghatti, DAMAC, Preston, Imtiaz and Vision Developments in 2026.

## Putting a number on a DLD fee waiver

The arithmetic is simple. The table below applies 4 per cent to the median off-plan prices Betterhomes reported in August 2026.

| Unit type | Median off-plan price (AED) | 4% DLD fee (AED) |
|---|---|---|
| Apartment | 1,941,130 | 77,645 |
| Villa | 6,791,380 | 271,655 |

Source: [Betterhomes, August 2026](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-news/dubai-developers-bring-back-4-dld-waivers-and-buyer-incentives). Fee figures match 4 per cent of each price.

The useful comparison is against a straight discount. Suppose a developer offered the apartment above at 4 per cent off instead of a waiver. The price would fall by AED 77,645, and the 4 per cent registration fee would then be charged on the lower figure, saving a further AED 3,106 (my calculation). A discount is therefore slightly better in cash terms than a waiver of the same headline value, and considerably better in everything priced off the sale value afterwards.

## What the waiver does not change

Three things stay anchored to the full price.

**The valuation basis.** Lenders commonly work from the contract price and their own valuation. A waiver does not lower the price, so any figure derived from it stays where it was. Ask your lender how they treat the price, not just the fee.

**The resale baseline.** An investor who later sells is comparing their unit with neighbours that were sold at the list price, with or without a waiver. A buyer who got a discount has a lower cost base to defend. A buyer who got a waiver paid the full price and received a one-off cash saving.

**The comparison between developers.** A waiver can make a project look cheaper on day one than it is. Two towers with the same payment plan are not equivalent if one comes with a waiver and the other with a better unit price. The only fair test is total cash cost over the life of the plan, which means adding the fee back to any project that does not waive it.

## The fine print worth reading

Betterhomes notes that a waiver is a developer promotion, so its terms vary by project, unit type and booking window, and resale properties are generally not eligible. Overseas buyers may qualify depending on the project terms. The same source says promotions can be time-limited and change without notice, and recommends that the buyer get the waiver amount confirmed in writing.

That last step matters more than it sounds. The waiver should appear in the reservation form or the sale and purchase agreement as a stated amount or a stated percentage, not as a verbal assurance from a sales agent. Ask three things:

1. Does the waiver cover the full 4 per cent, or only the buyer's share of a split?
2. Are other registration charges, such as the Oqood and administrative fees, included or separate? Betterhomes mentions an Oqood fee waiver only for one project, so do not assume it travels with the rest.
3. What happens to the waiver if the unit is reassigned before handover?

## How to weigh it against everything else

A waiver is one line in a larger comparison. The questions that decide whether a project suits a buyer are the developer's delivery record, the payment schedule, the escrow protection and the unit price per square foot against comparable stock. A 4 per cent saving can be wiped out by a premium of the same size in the price per square foot, or by a payment plan that front-loads cash the buyer would rather keep.

A reasonable rule is to convert every incentive into the same unit before comparing. Express the waiver as a percentage of price, express any price difference as a percentage, and add them. If one project is 3 per cent dearer per square foot but waives the fee, the net is a 1 per cent saving, which is too small to outweigh a weaker delivery record.

That is also where cross-developer comparison earns its keep. Incentives are designed to be compared inside one developer's brochure. They have to be compared across several to mean anything.
