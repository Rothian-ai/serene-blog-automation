Off-plan mortgage pre-approval is a bank's written indication, given at the point of booking, of how much it is prepared to lend against a unit that does not yet exist. It is not a loan, it does not release money during construction, and it is not a guarantee that the bank will lend on handover day. What it does is settle the affordability question early, which used to be left until the final months of a project.

In the 2026 offers, the figures are consistent. [Emaar Development and ADCB](https://www.emaar.com/en/press-release-listing/emaar-development-and-adcb-form-strategic-collaboration-to-transform-the-home-buying-experience-with-innovative-financing-solutions) describe pre-approval for financing of up to 50 per cent of a property's value, valid for twelve months and renewable annually until handover. The mortgage covers the handover payment. Everything paid to the developer before then comes from the buyer's own funds.

So the useful question is not whether a bank will pre-approve you. It is what the pre-approval fixes, what it leaves floating, and what you will have paid in cash by the time the bank is asked to lend.

## What changed in 2026

Traditionally, most off-plan buyers in Dubai relied on the developer's payment plan and approached a bank in the last months before completion. The product has moved earlier. On 16 April 2026, [Dubai Holding Real Estate and Emirates NBD signed a memorandum of understanding](https://dp.ae/article/191/dubai-holding-real-estate-and-emirates-nbd-partner-to-introduce-integrated-off-plan-mortgage-financing-across-meraas-nakheel-and-dubai-properties/) to offer integrated mortgage financing across Meraas, Nakheel and Dubai Properties developments, open to residents and non-residents, subject to approval. The release cites Dubai Land Department data showing more than 270,000 transactions worth AED 917 billion in 2025, with off-plan above 70 per cent of residential transactions.

The scale explains the shift. When roughly three in four residential sales are off-plan, a financing journey that begins only at handover leaves most buyers without a bank's view for years. [Reporting on the 2026 market](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-07-27:newsml_ZawjKd2L:0-dubai-off-plan-market-matures-as-buyers-target-value-delivery-records/) records bank and developer tie-ups of this kind with Emirates NBD, Mashreq, Dubai Islamic Bank, Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank and First Abu Dhabi Bank among the lenders offering off-plan frameworks.

## What off-plan mortgage pre-approval actually fixes

A pre-approval is an assessment of you, not of the building. The bank reviews income, liabilities and credit history, and states a ceiling. That fixes three things:

- **Your borrowing limit at the time of booking**, so the size of the deposit and the construction-stage instalments can be planned against a known number.
- **A rate or a rate basis.** The ADCB and Emaar offer quotes [rates from 3.49 per cent a year, fixed for three years, for a limited period](https://www.emaar.com/en/press-release-listing/emaar-development-and-adcb-form-strategic-collaboration-to-transform-the-home-buying-experience-with-innovative-financing-solutions), with no processing or valuation fees. A limited-period rate is a feature of the offer, not of the pre-approval, and it should be checked against what applies on the day the loan is drawn.
- **Which bank you are dealing with.** Many of these products are tied to a developer. That is convenient, and it is also a narrowing of choice.

## What it leaves open

Three things stay open until handover, and they matter more than the headline rate.

**The valuation.** The bank lends against the value of the finished unit at completion, not the price in the sales and purchase agreement. If the market has moved down, the loan can be smaller than the pre-approval implied, and the gap is paid in cash.

**Your circumstances.** Pre-approval lasts twelve months in these offers and is renewed annually. Each renewal is a fresh look. A change of job, a new liability or a lower income can change the answer in year three.

**The rate.** A fixed period in a 2026 offer protects the first years of a loan that starts at handover. It does not protect the rate you will be offered if the pre-approval has lapsed.

Notice what the table does not show: a single product where the bank pays for the first half of the building. The 50 per cent ceiling is the bank's, and it applies to the loan. The other half, plus the costs around it, is the buyer's.

## The cash you still carry

Press coverage of the market describes the working rule: [buyers fund their payments themselves until they reach 50 per cent equity, and some banks release funds once a project is 30 to 40 per cent complete](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-07-27:newsml_ZawjKd2L:0-dubai-off-plan-market-matures-as-buyers-target-value-delivery-records/). Loans then convert to mortgages of up to 25 years after handover. Treat these as the general pattern rather than a term every bank offers; the DIB structure above, with milestone-linked disbursement, is the exception that shows how much products vary.

The practical consequence is that a pre-approval can look generous while the buyer's actual cash requirement is large and front-loaded. A buyer who reads "50 per cent financing" as "50 per cent less to find" will be surprised by the instalments that fall due long before any bank is involved.

## Questions to ask before relying on one

A pre-approval is only as good as its conditions. Four are worth asking for in writing:

1. Is the lending limit a percentage of the purchase price or of the valuation at completion?
2. What happens at each annual renewal, and is the rate reset?
3. Is the product tied to this developer, and can the pre-approval be used with another bank if the terms change?
4. Which payments does the bank fund, and which must come from the buyer's own money?

## Where the risk sits

Pre-approval moves one risk forward in time: whether you can borrow. It does nothing for the others. Whether the project is delivered on schedule, whether the developer meets its milestones and whether the unit is finished to the contracted standard are all matters of record, and all independent of what a bank has said about you. A bank's interest in a project begins when you ask it to lend. Yours begins on the day you pay the deposit and continues long after the keys change hands.

That is the reason to read a pre-approval as one input among several. It answers a question about you. The questions about the developer, the escrow account and the building still need their own evidence, gathered independently of whoever is arranging the finance.
