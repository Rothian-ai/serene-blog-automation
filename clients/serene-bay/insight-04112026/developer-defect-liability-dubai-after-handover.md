Developer defect liability in Dubai has two statutory clocks. Under Article 40 of Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019, the developer must repair structural defects for ten years from the date of the project's completion certificate, and must repair or replace defective installations such as mechanical, electrical, sanitary and sewerage works for one year, generally from handover of your unit. That is the answer. Everything else in this piece is about which clock is running for you, what proves a claim, and what does not protect you.

Both periods are described in [Awatif Al Khouri's analysis for Chambers and Partners, dated 27 September 2026](https://chambers.com/articles/construction-defects-dubai-legal-remedies-for-new-property-owners), and the same two periods appear in [Afridi & Angell's summary of the law](https://afridi-angell.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/c_399Law-6-of-2019_On-the-Ownership-of-Common-Property-in-the-Emirate-of-Dubai.pdf). Neither is a substitute for your own contract, but both tell you where the floor sits.

## Developer defect liability in Dubai: the two clocks side by side

The law separates the building's structure from the things bolted into it. The split matters because the starting point differs, and so does the length.

| | Structural defects | Defective installations |
|---|---|---|
| Period | Ten years | One year |
| Starts | Date of the project's completion certificate | Handover of the unit to the owner (the completion certificate if the owner has not taken possession, per Chambers) |
| Covers | Foundations, columns, structural walls, ceilings, staircases, facades, roofs | Mechanical, electrical, sanitary and sewerage installations |
| Source | Article 40, Law No. 6 of 2019 | Article 40, Law No. 6 of 2019 |

Sources: [Chambers and Partners, 27 September 2026](https://chambers.com/articles/construction-defects-dubai-legal-remedies-for-new-property-owners); [Afridi & Angell](https://afridi-angell.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/c_399Law-6-of-2019_On-the-Ownership-of-Common-Property-in-the-Emirate-of-Dubai.pdf).

Commentators describe the one-year start point in slightly different words. Afridi & Angell tie it to handing the unit to the owner. Chambers says it generally runs from handover and, where the owner has not taken possession, from the completion certificate. Check the exact wording in the official text before you rely on a date.

## Why the start date is easy to lose

The ten-year clock begins at the completion certificate, which the authority issues for the project, not for your unit. A buyer who takes handover a year after the certificate has already used a year of the ten. For structure the lost year rarely changes the outcome. For installations the start date can decide the claim.

The one-year period is short and it is where most day-to-day faults sit: an air conditioning unit that fails, a water heater, a pump, a leaking waste pipe. Write down the handover date on the day you receive the keys. Everything is counted from it.

## The 5% holdback is not your warranty

A common assumption is that the developer's money is held back for a year to pay for defects. The record does not say that. Article 14 of [Dubai Law No. 8 of 2007](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2007/Law%20No.%20(8)%20of%202007.html) requires the escrow agent to retain five per cent of the total value of each escrow account once the developer obtains the completion certificate. The retained amount is released to the developer one year after the units are registered in the names of the purchasers.

The article does not state a purpose for the retention. It is a holdback inside the project escrow account. It is not a fund you can draw on, and it is not evidence of a defect claim. Some marketing pages describe it as a twelve-month post-handover guarantee; the statute text does not say so.

## A second layer: the Civil Code

Dubai's law sits beside the federal Civil Code. [Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/decennial-liability-uae-construction-2026-civil-code) report that Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 took effect on 1 June 2026 and repealed the 1985 code. Its contracting provisions sit in Articles 812 to 839, and the earlier Articles 880 to 883 on ten-year liability continue to be cited for older projects.

Under that regime, contractors and supervising engineers are jointly liable for ten years for collapse or defects that threaten stability or safety. Kayrouz states that the liability cannot be excluded or limited by contract, and that the parties may extend the period but not shorten it. Chambers adds that a claim must be brought within three years of the collapse or the discovery of the defect.

Two periods are at work here, and they do different jobs.

- **Ten years of liability.** The window in which the defect must appear.
- **Three years to sue.** The window, once you know, in which you must act.

Both sources are law-firm commentary rather than the statute. They agree with each other, which is useful, but neither replaces the official text.

## What this means for a defect list

A defect list made at handover is the first piece of evidence. A defect found in month nine is the second. For installations the question the statute poses is whether the item is defective within the period, so a dated, documented record does most of the work.

Chambers sets out what the file should hold:

1. Photographs and video, with the date each defect first appeared.
2. The Sale and Purchase Agreement, handover and snagging reports, and the specification.
3. Written notice to the developer, identifying the unit, the defect, the date found, earlier repair attempts and the remedy sought.
4. Where cause is disputed, an independent engineer's report. Chambers notes that courts may also appoint technical experts.

A phone call is not notice. An email with the snagging report attached is.

## What the law does not do for you

Nothing in the sources above sets a repair deadline, so the notice you send should name one. It does not turn cosmetic complaints into legal claims. A scratched worktop noticed after you moved in is a hard case to prove, which is one reason a documented inspection before final payment matters. The sequence is covered in [our note on independent snagging](/insights/why-independent-snagging-matters).

It also does not reach everything in the complex. Law No. 6 of 2019 concerns the jointly owned property, so the structural elements in the statutory list are the developer's. Ask which items in your own unit fall under it and which are your contract's promise alone. Chambers notes that the law's developer protections do not prejudice additional rights or warranties under other legislation, so a contractual warranty and the statutory one can apply together.

## Questions to put to the developer before you sign

1. Does the Sale and Purchase Agreement give a defects period longer than one year for installations?
2. From which date does the developer count it: handover, the completion certificate, or the date the unit is registered?
3. Where do disputes go, the courts or arbitration?
4. Who is the named contact for defect notices, and in what form must a notice be sent?

An answer in writing is worth more than a verbal one. A buyer who has these four answers before the keys change hands holds a clock that nobody can quietly restart.
