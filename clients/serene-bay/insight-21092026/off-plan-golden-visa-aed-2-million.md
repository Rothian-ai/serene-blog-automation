An off-plan buyer chasing the UAE's ten-year Golden Visa often assumes the test is simple: sign for a unit worth AED 2 million or more, and the Oqood contract does the rest. It does not. Both the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and Abu Dhabi's Department of Municipalities and Transport, through its Golden Visa channel, ask an applicant on a payment plan or a mortgage to prove what has actually reached the account, not what the sale contract says the unit is worth. A AED 2.5 million off-plan purchase with AED 900,000 paid so far does not clear the bar yet, whatever the contract price reads.

This distinction gets lost because the underlying rule genuinely did get easier. In January 2024, the requirement to have paid a fixed minimum of AED 1 million, or 50 per cent of the property's value, before applying was scrapped. That change was real and widely reported. What it removed, though, was a specific 50 per cent floor, not the principle that the applicant has to show money actually paid. The two are routinely conflated in marketing copy. Here is what the two authorities' own processes ask for.

## The 2024 easing, and what it actually changed

Before 2024, an investor buying on a mortgage or an instalment plan needed to show a minimum down payment of AED 1 million to qualify for the Golden Visa, on top of the underlying AED 2 million property value threshold, according to [The National's report on the change](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2024/01/24/golden-visa-dubai-property/). That fixed floor was dropped. [Khaleej Times reported](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/visa-and-immigration-in-uae/uae-golden-visa-dubai-drops-minimum-down-payment-required-for-property-owners) a real estate executive describing the result as "no minimum equity investment", and noted that off-plan units "could be considered on a case-to-case basis", with applicants submitting the developer's sale letter or a mortgage document alongside a passport copy.

What did not change is the AED 2 million floor itself, or the requirement to evidence the money behind it. Removing a fixed 50 per cent rule is not the same as removing the need to show a paid-in amount, and the two land departments' current application processes make that difference concrete.

## What Dubai's process asks for

The [DLD's own Golden Visa investor service page](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-golden-visa-investor/) states the underlying threshold plainly: the property must be worth AED 2 million, "wholly owned by the investor (one or more properties)". For a property that carries a mortgage, the same page does not treat the contract value as sufficient on its own. It asks for "a no-objection bank letter... indicating the paid amount and the balance", and specifies that when a property is mortgaged, the applicant needs "a bank letter indicating 2 million AED paid amount as a proof".

[Dubai's General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA)](https://www.gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/8ea80da4-f43e-11eb-0320-0050569629e8) runs the residency side of the same application and asks for a property status certificate from DLD confirming the total value, alongside a requirement to place a lien on the property "to ensure the continuity of ownership throughout the validity of the Golden Residency". Neither page treats an off-plan Oqood contract's face value as self-certifying; both point back to a certified value and, where financing is involved, a paid-amount confirmation from the bank.

## What Abu Dhabi's process asks for

Abu Dhabi is more explicit about the off-plan route specifically. The [Abu Dhabi Golden Visa page for real estate investors](https://www.added.gov.ae/en/live/long-term-residency/abu-dhabi-golden-visa/Investors/visa-for-real-estate-investors) sets out three documentation paths depending on how the property is held:

- **Fully owned**: a registration agreement or a Real Estate Unit Value Certificate from the Department of Municipalities and Transport.
- **Mortgaged**: the same value certificate, a search certificate confirming the property is unencumbered beyond the mortgage, and confirmation that the investor's equity, the unmortgaged share, is at least AED 2,000,000. A property worth AED 5 million cannot carry a mortgage above AED 3 million and still qualify on that unit alone.
- **Off-plan**: a purchase agreement from an approved developer, plus proof that "no less than AED 2,000,000 in payments till date have been made".

That last line is the clearest statement either emirate publishes: for an off-plan unit, the number that matters is cumulative payments made, not the price on the sale contract.

## Dubai and Abu Dhabi side by side

| | Dubai (DLD / GDRFA) | Abu Dhabi (ADDED) |
|---|---|---|
| Base threshold | AED 2,000,000 property value | AED 2,000,000 property value |
| Fully owned, no mortgage | Value certified by DLD; lien placed for the visa term | Value certificate from the Department of Municipalities and Transport |
| Mortgaged | Bank NOC confirming amount paid and balance; letter must show AED 2,000,000 paid | Bank confirmation that unmortgaged equity is at least AED 2,000,000 |
| Off-plan | Developer letter or mortgage document; DLD treats value as needing a paid-amount proof once financed | Developer purchase agreement plus proof of AED 2,000,000 paid to date |
| Visa term | 10 years, renewable | 10 years, renewable |

## Why the simpler version keeps circulating

The "just needs AED 2 million on paper" version of the rule is not fabricated from nothing. It is a reasonable-sounding compression of a real, reported change: the fixed AED 1 million or 50 per cent down payment rule genuinely disappeared in 2024, and a buyer no longer needs to clear that specific bar before applying. The compression happens when "no fixed 50 per cent rule" becomes "the amount paid does not matter". Those are different claims, and only the first one is what changed.

An off-plan buyer relying on the second version can end up assembling an application around the Oqood's contract price, only to be asked by the bank or the land department for a paid-amount confirmation that a payment plan two or three instalments in cannot yet supply.

## What this means while a payment plan is still running

For a unit bought on a staged schedule, whether that is a percentage-per-month structure or a plan weighted toward completion, the AED 2 million test is a moving target that tracks the payment schedule, not a box ticked at signing. Two things follow from how both authorities structure the requirement:

1. **Track cumulative payments, not the contract figure.** The relevant number is what has actually left the buyer's account and reached the developer or the escrow account, which is the figure a developer's payment statement or a bank NOC will show.
2. **Keep the paperwork as it happens.** Developer payment receipts, bank confirmation letters, and, once obtained, a certificate of Oqood or unit value from the land department are the documents that later fill the "proof of amount paid" line in either emirate's process. Assembling them at the point of applying, months or years after early payments, is harder than keeping them as each instalment clears.

A buyer who reaches AED 2 million in cumulative payments on a AED 3.5 million off-plan unit is, on the documentation both emirates publish, closer to eligible than one who has a AED 2.5 million contract and AED 600,000 paid, even though the second contract is nominally larger.

## Where the two routes diverge

Abu Dhabi's published process draws a sharper line between "fully owned", "mortgaged" and "off-plan" than Dubai's does, and states the off-plan paid-amount figure in as many words. Dubai's GDRFA and DLD pages describe the underlying value threshold and the mortgage documentation but do not carry a page addressed to off-plan Oqood holders specifically in the same terms, which is consistent with Khaleej Times's reporting that Dubai considers off-plan cases individually rather than against a published off-plan checklist. That gap is worth knowing before assuming either emirate's off-plan process will read exactly like the other's.
