A bank in the UAE will lend at most half the price of an off-plan unit, whatever the buyer's nationality, income or the size of the deal. The Central Bank's loan-to-value cap on an off-plan mortgage has stood at 50 per cent [since Circular 31/2013](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-central-bank-issues-new-mortgage-cap-rules), and it applies uniformly: a UAE national and an expatriate face the same ceiling, on a studio or a penthouse. That is roughly half of what the same buyer could borrow on a completed home. Major UAE banks currently offer first-time buyers up to 85 per cent financing for UAE nationals and up to 80 per cent for expatriates on [ready property](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/mortgage-loans-from-uae-banks/). Off-plan, both figures collapse to 50. The reason is not the buyer's file. It is the asset: a bank cannot inspect, independently value in the ordinary way, or repossess and resell a unit that does not exist yet. Off-plan accounted for roughly 70 per cent of Dubai's residential transactions in the first quarter of 2026, so this ceiling is not a footnote. It is the financing reality most buyers in the market this year are working within, and it is the reason a developer's payment plan, not a mortgage, carries most off-plan purchases through to handover.

## Why the off-plan mortgage cap sits where it does

An off-plan purchase registers on Dubai Land Department's interim Oqood register at signature, not the full title register. It converts to a title deed only once the unit is built and handed over. Until then, what the buyer holds is a contractual right to receive a specific unit from a specific developer, not a completed, saleable asset. A bank underwriting a ready home can send a valuer to walk the property, compare it against recent sales nearby, and if the loan defaults, take possession and sell it. None of that exists for a slab that has not been poured. The 50 per cent cap prices that gap: whatever happens to the project between signature and handover, the bank's exposure is capped at half the contract price, and the buyer's own funds absorb the first half of any shortfall.

## The borrowing gap, in one table

| Purchase stage | Buyer | Maximum loan-to-value | Minimum cash from the buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off-plan, any price | UAE national or expatriate | 50% | 50% |
| Ready property, first mortgage | UAE national | up to 85% | from 15% |
| Ready property, first mortgage | Expatriate | up to 80% | from 20% |

Sources: [Khaleej Times on the Central Bank's off-plan cap](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-central-bank-issues-new-mortgage-cap-rules); [current bank loan-to-value terms via MyBayut](https://www.bayut.com/mybayut/mortgage-loans-from-uae-banks/).

A second constraint sits underneath both rows. The Central Bank also caps the [debt-burden ratio](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/help-and-support/your-debt-burden-ratio) at 50 per cent of gross monthly income across every debt a borrower carries, mortgage included. A buyer who clears the loan-to-value hurdle can still be turned down, or offered a smaller loan, if the repayment would push their total monthly debt past that line.

## Why the cap rarely bites at the point of signature

Most off-plan buyers do not feel the 50 per cent ceiling when they sign, because they are not applying for a mortgage yet. A typical construction-linked plan asks for a reservation deposit, a series of instalments tied to build progress, and defers a large final slice to handover, sometimes with a post-handover tail running one to five years beyond it. The buyer is paying the developer directly through most of the build. The mortgage, if there is one, is arranged near completion, against whatever balance is still owed. That is a deliberate feature of the plan structures on offer in 2026, where 70/30 and 80/20 construction-to-handover splits are common on new launches, alongside plans that push 40 to 60 per cent of the price into the years after keys change hands.

## Where it does bite: the final stretch

The cap becomes real the moment a buyer tries to convert a remaining balance into a bank loan close to handover. If the outstanding amount is more than half the unit's assessed value, the bank will not cover the gap. The buyer needs to fund the difference from savings, a sale, or a private arrangement, on a timeline set by the developer's completion notice rather than the buyer's own. Valuation risk compounds it. A bank lends against the lower of the agreed price and its own valuer's figure, so if the market has softened since the unit was booked, the 50 per cent is 50 per cent of a smaller number than the buyer expects.

## Common questions

### Does a larger income change the 50 per cent cap?

No. The cap is set on the value of the property, not on the borrower's income or credit history. A higher income can help a buyer qualify for the full size of loan the cap allows, and it feeds into the debt-burden ratio test, but it cannot lift the loan-to-value ceiling itself. A buyer earning twice as much as another still faces the same 50 per cent line.

### Can a personal loan cover the rest of the down payment?

No, and this is worth stating plainly because it is the workaround buyers ask about most. [Central Bank rules require the down payment to come from the borrower's own resources, not from another loan or a credit card](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-property-law-mortgage-financing-personal-loan-down-payment). A structure that uses borrowed money to plug the gap between the mortgage and the price does not meet that requirement, and a bank underwriting the mortgage will typically decline to proceed once it identifies the source.

### Is the cap different for a buyer investing from outside the UAE?

The 50 per cent ceiling itself is the same. What changes is access: fewer lenders will underwrite an off-plan mortgage for a non-resident at all, and those that do often set additional conditions. For a buyer purchasing from abroad, the practical funding gap can end up wider than the regulatory minimum, simply because fewer banks are competing for the loan.

## What this means for comparing payment plans

This is where the mortgage cap turns into a question worth asking before reservation, not after: which developer's payment plan actually reduces the cash a buyer needs at handover, once the 50 per cent ceiling is factored in. A steep post-handover tail looks generous on the brochure page. It only works if the buyer can either fund the final balance in cash or is confident a bank will lend against at least half of it when the time comes, and a bank's own valuation, not the developer's price list, decides that. Two units at the same headline price, from two different developers, on two different payment structures, can leave a buyer needing very different amounts of cash on the same date. Working out that gap across developers, rather than assuming any plan converts neatly into a mortgage at the end, is a large part of what cross-developer advice is for.

## The Dubai market this cap now sits under

Off-plan sales made up around 70 per cent of both transaction volume and value in Dubai in the first quarter of 2026, with March alone recording more than 10,300 off-plan deals worth AED 31.2 billion, [according to Q1 2026 figures reported by Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-property-sales-reach-dh1767-billion-in-q1-2026-off-plan-demand-and-prices-hold-firm-1.500495516). Across the wider market, cash still dominates: mortgage-backed sales totalled AED 179.3 billion of Dubai's roughly AED 686.8 billion in property transaction value in 2025, [about a quarter of the market by value, with the average loan-to-value on those deals falling to just under 73 per cent](https://www.agbi.com/real-estate/2026/01/dubai-property-boom-leans-on-cash-as-mortgage-lending-lags/). Off-plan mortgages sit at the more conservative end of that blended average, not above it.

## Planning for the cap, not around it

None of this makes an off-plan mortgage unusual, or a reason to avoid the segment. It is simply a different financing shape from a ready home, and one worth planning for from the reservation deposit onward rather than discovering at handover. The practical version of the rule is straightforward: budget for at least half the price in cash or developer instalments, treat any bank estimate given before a formal valuation as provisional, and read a payment plan by the balance it leaves at completion, not by the size of the discount advertised at launch.
