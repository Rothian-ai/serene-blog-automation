Dubai runs two residency routes tied to owning property, and they no longer share a common rule of thumb. The two-year property investor visa, aimed at a buyer who owns a home outright, dropped its minimum property value entirely for a sole owner in 2026: the property previously had to be worth at least AED 750,000, and now there is no floor at all, provided the property is legally registered with the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and fully owned by one person. A buyer who holds a property jointly with someone else still faces a threshold, but a lower one than before: AED 400,000 per owner, down from AED 750,000 each ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/visa-and-immigration-in-uae/dubai-property-investor-visa-rules-removes-minimum-property-value-requirement); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-scraps-minimum-property-value-for-solo-investor-visas-sets-joint-ownership-floor-what-it-means-1.500523065)). The separate ten-year Golden Visa, the route used for a larger real estate stake, kept its AED 2,000,000 minimum and was not part of this change ([Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-05-01_75/en)).

With off-plan sales running at 76 per cent of all Dubai property transactions between April and June 2026, on Dubai Land Department figures reported by Reuters ([TradingView/Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-07-27:newsml_ZawjKd2L:0-dubai-off-plan-market-matures-as-buyers-target-value-delivery-records/)), most buyers weighing a purchase against a residency goal are buying something that has not been built yet. How the ownership on the paperwork is structured now decides more about who qualifies for what than the price tag on the unit does.

## Two routes, two different jobs

The property investor visa and the Golden Visa answer different questions. The two-year visa is a residency benefit attached to owning a home in Dubai; the Golden Visa is a longer-term status built around a larger capital commitment, of which real estate is one qualifying route.

| Route | Duration | Minimum property value | Ownership condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property investor visa | 2 years, renewable | None for a sole owner; AED 400,000 per owner if held jointly | Property legally registered with the Dubai Land Department |
| Golden Visa, real estate route | 10 years, renewable | AED 2,000,000 | Property fully owned by the investor |

## What actually changed, and when

The update reached the property investor visa through the Dubai Land Department's Cube platform in April 2026, and it was reported without a formal press release from DLD, GDRFA or the federal Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Ports Security authority ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/04/30/dubais-updated-property-visa-rules-to-spur-demand-and-attract-overseas-buyers/); [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-05-01_75/en)). Two things moved at once: the AED 750,000 floor disappeared completely for a sole owner, and the joint-ownership floor fell to AED 400,000 per person from the same AED 750,000 each that used to apply. Khaleej Times reported that demand sharpened immediately at the entry level, in studio and one-bedroom stock in communities such as Jumeirah Village Circle, Jumeirah Village Triangle, Dubai South and Arjan, where prices commonly sit below the old threshold ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/2-year-property-visa-dubai-sees-rising-demand-for-dh750000-homes-after-rule-change)).

## Why the ownership structure now matters more than the price

Before the change, two people buying together each had to individually clear AED 750,000, a combined AED 1,500,000 minimum for both to qualify. Under the current rule, two joint owners each need AED 400,000, a combined AED 800,000, while a single owner needs nothing beyond registering the property in their own name. That gap means the same couple buying the same unit can get a materially different outcome depending on whether the title is registered to one name or split between two, which is now a structuring question worth asking before signing rather than after.

## The Golden Visa's separate arithmetic

The Golden Visa's AED 2,000,000 threshold sits outside this reform entirely and is administered federally through the Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Ports Security authority, which lists the requirement as full ownership of property worth at least AED 2,000,000 ([ICP](https://icp.gov.ae/en/services-details/?serviceid=68e34eea5ae59b00117383d5)). That threshold, and the ten-year term attached to it, applies the same way to a purchase in Abu Dhabi as it does in Dubai, which is not true of the two-year visa reform described above.

## What "legally registered" means for an off-plan purchase

The reporting on the 2026 change specifies that the property must be legally registered with the Land Department, without spelling out what stage of construction that requires ([Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/news/2026-05-01_75/en)). In Dubai, an off-plan unit is entered on the DLD's Oqood interim register from the point of sale, ahead of the title deed that follows handover. Whether Oqood registration alone satisfies the two-year visa's condition, or whether GDRFA expects the completed title deed, is not stated consistently across the reporting to date. A buyer timing an application around an off-plan purchase should confirm the registration stage requirement directly with GDRFA or DLD before assuming eligibility starts at exchange rather than at handover.

## Common questions

### Does this rule change apply outside Dubai?

No. The property investor visa reform reported here runs through Dubai's own Land Department and residency platforms. Abu Dhabi's equivalent programme is administered separately through ADREC, and nothing in the current reporting extends the AED 750,000 removal there. The Golden Visa's AED 2,000,000 route is the exception, since it is federal.

### Can a buyer combine several smaller units to reach the Golden Visa's threshold?

The Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Ports Security authority's own guidance allows the AED 2,000,000 to be met across more than one property rather than in a single purchase, as long as each is fully owned by the applicant ([ICP](https://icp.gov.ae/en/services-details/?serviceid=68e34eea5ae59b00117383d5)). That is a different mechanism from the joint-ownership rule on the two-year visa, which concerns shared title on a single property rather than combined value across several.

A buyer comparing developments with a residency outcome in mind is, in practice, comparing three separate numbers at once: the unit price, the ownership structure on the title, and which of the two visa routes that structure actually clears. None of the three moves together, and the paperwork now rewards checking all three before reserving a unit rather than after.
